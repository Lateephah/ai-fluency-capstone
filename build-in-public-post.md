I just shipped my capstone for FlyRank's AI Fluency internship: a portfolio built around one real product, LexGuard, a tool that reads a contract and flags the risky parts in plain language, so an SME owner doesn't have to click "I agree" blind.

The harder part wasn't building LexGuard. It was deciding what NOT to put on the page with it.

I came into this wanting my portfolio to show everything, every model I'd trained, every domain I'd touched. I got pushed, hard, to cut that down to one claim, one project, one specific person I'm trying to reach. It felt like underselling myself at the time.

Here's the real decision I want to name: I have a second case study already fully drafted and ready to publish, a content-ranking model from my ML internship. I'm not adding it yet. It answers a different question for a different audience than LexGuard does, and bolting it on would recreate the exact vagueness I just spent weeks cutting out.

So the honest limitation in my portfolio right now: it can't yet show range. It shows depth in one direction, on purpose, and nothing else. The real next step for me isn't "add more projects", it is grouping what I've already built, picking one direction to actually go deep in, and letting the next claim get bolder because it's backed by more than one project in that same lane.
